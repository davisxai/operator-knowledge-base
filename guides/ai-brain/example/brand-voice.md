# Brand voice: OperatorOS

How OperatorOS sounds, how it is named, when it is mentioned, and when it stays out. Kept separate from character-bible.md on purpose. Davis is who the reader follows. OperatorOS is where the work happens. Products are what Davis uses to do the work.

## What it is

- Solo AI and automation services agency, founded and run by Davis McMurrain.
- Builds AI agents, voice systems, lead gen tools, RAG pipelines, websites, and automation workflows for clients.
- Core principle: build in the client's environment from day one. The client owns every account.

## What we sound like

- Clear, specific, outcome-led, no hype. State the outcome plainly, with a number, and let the work be the proof.
- Plain words. "Use" not "utilize." Short sentences. One idea each.
- Outcomes over features. Specific over vague. Customer language over company language.
- Active voice. Qualifiers cut. "Here is what it does," never "we think we can probably help."
- Pricing is a statement, not a negotiation. The number in bold, no justification paragraph. The section header is "Investment."
- Ownership gets said plainly: everything we build, you own.

## What we never say

- Banned: game-changer, revolutionary, cutting-edge, seamless, unlock, supercharge, 10x, next-level, "the future of", leverage, delve, robust, elevate, "in today's fast-paced world."
- No emojis. No exclamation cadence. No em dashes, no double dashes.

## How we are named

- "OperatorOS". Proper case, one word. Never "Operator OS", never all caps, never "Inc." after it.
- Automated work is referenced as "Operator", never by the model name.
- The title line under Davis's name is always "Founder | OperatorOS".

## When the company is mentioned

- Light mention only. The bio is the primary placement.
- In content, one sentence when the story needs it: "I run an agency called OperatorOS." Never a pitch.
- Framed as the context for the work, the place client engagements happen. Not something the reader should buy.

## When it stays out

- Educational pieces that work without it. Carousels unless directly relevant. Anything that would read as a sales pitch.
- Before any piece that references the business: is OperatorOS doing work in this piece, or is it name-dropped? If dropped, cut it.
- Tools are talked about globally and named specifically, as a peer, not a vendor. No single product is positioned as the signature offering.
